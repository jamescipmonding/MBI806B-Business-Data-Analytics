# Insights and Findings

## Key Insights
The analysis revealed clear long-term upward price trends across
several major stocks, supported by sustained momentum over
multiple years. Moving averages highlighted periods of acceleration
and consolidation, allowing trends to be identified beyond
short-term price fluctuations.

Comparative analysis showed that certain stocks exhibited higher
volatility without proportionate increases in return, reinforcing
the importance of risk-adjusted evaluation rather than relying on
price performance alone.

---

## Valuation Interpretation
Indicative valuation metrics suggested that stocks frequently traded
close to their calculated fair value, with temporary deviations
representing short-term market sentiment rather than fundamental
mispricing.

Periods where prices diverged significantly from longer-term moving
averages were often followed by mean reversion, supporting the use
of moving averages as a practical indicative valuation tool.

---

## Risk and Momentum Observations
Momentum indicators highlighted shifts in short-term and long-term
trends, with bearish signals typically emerging during periods of
increased volatility.

Risk and return analysis confirmed that higher volatility did not
consistently result in higher returns, underscoring the need for
balanced decision-making when assessing investment opportunities.

---

## Limitations
This analysis relies on historical price data and does not account
for external macroeconomic factors, company-specific fundamentals,
or unexpected market events.

Indicative valuation measures are not predictive and should be used
as decision-support tools rather than definitive pricing models.

---

## Recommendations and Next Steps
Future analysis could incorporate fundamental indicators such as
earnings, revenue growth, or macroeconomic variables to enhance
valuation accuracy.

Additional machine learning techniques may be applied to explore
pattern recognition or anomaly detection, further strengthening
decision-support capabilities.
